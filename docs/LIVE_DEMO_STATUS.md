## The intended hacker/fixer loop

1. `envs.yaml` selects CPU-scored Hub environments and their API/version-specific runtimes.
   `scoring.inspect_env` obtains real examples, references and grader source in the env's
   own interpreter; `scoring.score` uses the frozen checker's worker to execute rewards.
2. `roles.probe` is the hacker. It sees reward source, task examples and previous scores,
   and proposes three concrete completions with mechanism IDs and exploit classes.
3. A high reward is only a candidate exploit. `strict_verdict` checks correctness against
   ground truth. Uncertain cases go to the independent `roles.judge`. Only a completion
   that scores at least 80% of its passing reference and is independently incorrect may
   count as a confirmed false positive.
4. `roles.repair` is the fixer. `patching.py` embeds its tightening logic in a controlled
   wrapper. Re-score the candidate and previously found attacks, plus benign/reference
   examples. Reject scoring errors, benign regressions, or failed patch-integrity checks.
5. Repeat within round and dollar caps. Persist attempts, verdicts, iteration records,
   replay files, runtime/source/dataset fingerprints, spend, and SQLite invariants.
6. Build the evidence-based leaderboard, reconcile invariants and run
   `scripts/check_leaderboard.py`. Push validated records and trigger graph maintenance.
7. For independent targets, retrieve applicable knowledge and run target-specific attack
   and benign validation before recording transfer outcomes. Never pre-harden an audited
   leaderboard source and then claim its original reward was measured unchanged.

The role/scoring/patching/verification helpers exist. **The orchestration is not complete:**
`harden/audit/loop.py` stops at a docstring-only `Audit.verify`; it lacks setup, the driver,
evidence finalization and the CLI entry point. The file's introductory command is not
currently runnable as a functioning audit. Do not claim that it ran N Hub environments.

## Prepared Hub experiment

`python3 -m harden.experiments.hub_pilot --output results/hub_pilot_plan.json` performs
no environment loading or API/model calls. The checked-in report makes current blockers
machine-specific rather than repeating old notes from another developer's setup.

Initial proposed N = 3:

| Environment | Why include it | Runtime contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `primeintellect/reverse-text` | Transformation correctness versus a similarity reward | legacy, `.venvs/hub` |
| `primeintellect/ascii-tree` | Structured formatting and multi-component partial credit | legacy, `.venvs/v012` |
| `primeintellect/math500` | Boxed-answer math verifier as a stricter comparison | v1, `.venvs/hub` |

Use a two-round capped pilot first, then decide whether to finish the full acceptance
protocol (up to five rounds). **M is an observed result**, deduplicated by mechanism per
environment; report zero findings or failed loads honestly. The plan excludes known
broken installs such as base64bench's missing packaged data. Its source/reward descriptions
come from the repository's previously reviewed `envs.yaml`; actual package/dataset
versions must be pinned and rechecked on the machine that executes the experiment.
